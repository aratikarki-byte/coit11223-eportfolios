# e-portfolio-3-Intellectual-Property
A collection of artefacts that demonstrate what I have learnt about Intellectual Property this week

## Artefact 1: Who Owns Something an AI Helped Create?

 https://doi.org/10.1111/jwip.12310

### Summary of the artefact
The author Mazzi in his journal paper of 2024 (p. 411) discusses the issue of legality of who is the 'author' of an output generated using Artificial Intelligence through a text prompt created by a human. Mazzi uses the comparison of the output generated by the machine and the previous concepts of originality such as collage art, whereby there is selection and arrangement of materials without being able to create everything anew. Mazzi (2024, p. 415) states that originality can still exist in how a person crafts and refines a prompt, even though copyright legislation did not envisage machines.

### Justification on why I chose the artefact
The reason why I found this article compelling enough to choose it was because it helped me move on from the idea of whether AI can have copyright ownership to a more relevant question – how much creative control must a person have over the AI in order for the work produced to be his/her own creation. Since I have used AI in formulating my ideas for this very course, the difference has been quite personal to me.

## Artefact 2: Disney and Universal Sue Midjourney

https://www.npr.org/2025/06/12/nx-s1-5431684/ai-disney-universal-midjourney-copyright-infringement-lawsuit

### Summary of the artefact
According to Veltman (2025), the first lawsuit by major film studios in Hollywood against an artificial intelligence firm was lodged in June 2025 against the image generation AI Midjourney, alleging that the company trained its AI model using the characters owned by the film companies and allowing subscribers to create unlimited copies of the images without permission and remuneration.

### Justification on why I chose the artefact
I chose this article because it made intellectual property law feel real rather than theoretical. It showed me that Midjourney's tool did not create these characters from nothing, it learned from decades of studio-owned work, which connects to what I am learning about an ICT professional's responsibility to respect ownership when building or using data-driven systems.

## Artefact 3: How Generative AI Is Reshaping Copyright Law

https://www.euipo.europa.eu/en/publications/genai-from-a-copyright-perspective-2025

### Summary of the artefact
This 2025 study, commissioned by the European Union Intellectual Property Office, explains how generative AI complicates existing copyright frameworks at two separate stages: when copyrighted material is used to train a model, and when the model produces an output. The European Union Intellectual Property Office (2025) explains that regulators are still working out how to balance protecting original creators with allowing AI innovation to continue.

### Justification on why I chose the artefact
I found this report interesting since it gave me a holistic overview of the AI and IP policy, instead of just one company's court case. It made me realise this is a global governance problem, not something one lawsuit will settle. Reading a European source also made me consider that other regions besides the US can take different approaches to the same technology.

## Artefact 4: Can AI Own an Idea?

https://www.ie.edu/insights/videos/can-ai-own-an-idea/

### Summary of the artefact
Through this short video, Garrigues (2025) explains how the notion of protecting ideas has developed over time, from shared ideas in early societies to the present-day systems of copyright, trademarks, and patents which protect the creativity of the individual human being. To illustrate this point, Garrigues presents a concrete case in which an AI-generated picture wins an art contest but fails to be copyrighted due to a lack of human authorship.

### Justification on why I chose the artefact
The reason why I selected this video is that it described a difficult legal history in easily understandable words and thus made the comprehension of other, more complex texts easier. This particular perspective on history was quite new to me, as I never realised that IP law is a relatively modern invention based on the assumption that only humans could be creators of something. This made me think that today's AI ownership debate is only the continuation of a very old dispute about who should get recognition and reward for their creation.
