## Artefact 1: Scholarly Article

**Responsible governance of generative AI: conceptualizing GenAI as complex adaptive systems**

https://doi.org/10.1093/polsoc/puae040

### Summary of the artefact

The article by Janssen (2025) investigates how organisations govern generative AI, arguing that it should be understood as a complex adaptive system rather than a fixed, predictable tool. This means AI behaviour emerges from ongoing interaction between people, data, policies and the technology itself, not from a single set of rules that can be written once and left alone. Janssen (2025, p. 38) explains that many current AI governance approaches are "technology-deterministic," assuming the technology is static, when in reality it co-evolves with its social environment. The article concludes that no single mechanism is enough to govern generative AI responsibly, and that combinations of legal, technical, and societal mechanisms are needed, alongside joint accountability between the parties involved.

## Artefact 3: Video

**The Ethics of AI: Who Controls the Future?**

https://www.ie.edu/insights/videos/the-ethics-of-ai-who-controls-the-future/

### Summary of the artefact

In this video, researcher David Leslie discusses how the rapid rise of generative AI since the release of ChatGPT in 2022 has outpaced ethical and regulatory frameworks. He raises concerns about AI's potential misuse in areas such as security and disinformation, and questions whether technology this powerful, which affects the public interest, should be left in the control of private companies.
