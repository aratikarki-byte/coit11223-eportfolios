#e-portfolio-4-Censorship-and-Government

A collection of artefacts that demonstrate what I have learnt about Censorship and Government this week.

Artefact 1: Online Safety Act 2021
Australian Human Rights Commission – Online Safety Act 2021: Human Rights Review (2024)
https://humanrights.gov.au/resource-hub/by-resource-type/submissions/technology-and-human-rights/online-safety

###Summary of the artefact
Analysis of the Australian Human Rights Commission submission of 2024 is based on the statutory review of Australia’s Online Safety Act 2021. The topic covers the relevance of regulating online safety legislation, and the role of the eSafety Commissioner in responding to such online threats. The connection between the online safety and human rights in the form of freedom of expression is covered in this artefact. This artefact has helped me understand that governmental intervention in online platforms can address issues of harmful content, as well as questions of communication access for individuals (Australian Human Rights Commission, 2024).

###Justification on why I chose the artefact
This artefact was chosen as it shows how government regulation is related to censorship and freedom of expression. It has helped me realize that the topic of online safety goes beyond technology and legislation and is related to ethics and different stakeholders' interests in society. This artefact was chosen as it could be valuable for ICT practitioners as their work could be related to digital systems which could be regulated by the government.

 #Artefact 2: Combatting Misinformation and Disinformation Bill 2024
Australian Human Rights Commission – Combatting Misinformation and Disinformation Bill 2024 Submission
https://humanrights.gov.au/resource-hub/by-resource-type/submissions/technology-and-human-rights/combatting-misinformation-and-disinformation-bill-2024

###Summary of the artefact
In its 2024 submission, the Australian Human Rights Commission looks into the implications that may arise in relation to the human rights when it comes to the Combating Misinformation and Disinformation Bill 2024. In its submission, the Commission talks about how misinformation and disinformation may harm society and human rights, as well as the impact of regulations by the government on the right to freedom of expression (Australian Human Rights Commission, 2024).

###Justification on why I chose the artefact
This artefact was chosen as it links misinformation and disinformation with ICT and social media platforms. The artefact allowed me to see that there are ethical dilemmas that can arise in regulating online information, since decisions have to be made as to what information has to be limited or moderated. This relates to my unit, as ICT practitioners can be involved in processes such as distributing, moderating, or identifying online information. This artefact helped me to see that there were ethical questions relating to regulating communications using technology.

##Artefact 3: Social Media Minimum Age
Australian Human Rights Commission – Social Media Ban: Human Rights Impact 2025
https://humanrights.gov.au/our-work/submissions/technology-and-human-rights/social-media-ban

###Summary of the artefact
The submission made by the Australian Human Rights Commission in 2025 touches upon the issues related to human rights aspects associated with restrictions regarding the use of social media by individuals aged below 16 years. These restrictions aimed at protecting children and youths from possible harm done by the internet. At the same time, the submission examines the consequences of such restrictions in terms of freedom of expression, right to information, privacy and social participation (Australian Human Rights Commission, 2025)
