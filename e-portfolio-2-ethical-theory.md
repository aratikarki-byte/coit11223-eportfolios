A collection of artefacts that demonstrate what I have learnt about Ethical Theory this week.

## Artefact 1: Scholarly Article

Ethical theories, governance models, and strategic frameworks for responsible AI adoption and organizational success

https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/artificial-intelligence/articles/10.3389/frai.2025.1619029/full

### Summary of the artefact

The paper gives a critical examination of the application of utilitarianism, deontology, and virtue ethics in relation to the use of AI in organizations. Utilitarianism involves taking into account the outcomes of an action; deontology (also known as Kantianism), on the other hand, is the evaluation of an action based on the duty of the action and not its result; finally, virtue ethics involve asking oneself what a virtuous person would do (Madanchian and Taherdoost, 2025, p. 3). The paper also gives an explanation of the various models of governance used in organizations for the application of these theories.

### Justification on why I chose the artefact

I selected this piece of writing because it directly addresses the three main ethical theories which were highlighted during our Week 4 Workshop, including utilitarianism, Kantianism, and virtue ethics. The reading gave me an insight into how ethical theories are applied by organizations as opposed to individuals in making decisions concerning their operations, especially in using AI systems, something that helped me understand the App Release Dilemma and Highway Routing scenario in our Workshop.

## Artefact 2: News Article

Navigating the ethical dilemmas of self-driving cars: who decides when safety is at risk?

https://www.forbes.com/councils/forbestechcouncil/2024/10/23/navigating-the-ethical-dilemmas-of-self-driving-cars-who-decides-when-safety-is-at-risk/

### Summary of the artefact

In this piece in Forbes, the ethical conundrums associated with programming of self-driving cars are discussed through the use of a famous ethical puzzle known as the trolley problem. A utilitarian perspective which emphasizes the saving of the most number of lives is compared against the Kantian viewpoint where nobody can be harmed under any circumstances. This piece states that self-driving cars necessitate that programmers have to program these different perspectives on ethics within the software itself.

### Justification on why I chose the artefact

I chose this artefact because it applies the exact contrast between Kantianism and Act Utilitarianism covered in our workshop to a live, real-world ICT problem. Unlike the classroom's app-release or highway scenarios, this shows programmers making binding ethical decisions in code, which reinforced why ICT professionals need a working knowledge of ethical theory, not just technical skill, when building autonomous systems.

## Artefact 3: Video

Utilitarianism vs. Deontology: The Ethical Dilemma Explained

https://www.youtube.com/shorts/RrB6gbzfNZ4

### Summary of the artefact

The video introduces and compares utilitarianism and deontological (Kantian) ethics through the use of everyday moral scenarios. It provides a definition of utilitarianism, where an action is considered good or bad depending on its capacity to create maximum happiness for the majority, and deontological ethics, where an action is judged based on adherence to a moral duty or rule irrespective of its consequences.

### Justification on why I chose the artefact

I chose this artefact because it helped clarify the understanding of both theories prior to their application to the Kantian GenAI scenario and the Highway Routing utilitarianism scenario used during the class workshops. It enabled me to understand the distinction clearly through practical examples prior to applying the theories to ICT-related cases.
